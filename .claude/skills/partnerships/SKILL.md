# Partnerships

Some features are a partner's to give (filing a flight plan, authorising a drone flight, a fuel price), and some customers come by the hundred through one organisation. The owner signs every agreement; this skill finds the options and prepares the ask.

## 1. What the partner gives
Name the feature and what is outside the app's reach: an API key, a data licence, an approval, or a group of users. Check first whether a public source already does it (the `market-gap` skill): FAA and NWS data is free and needs no partner.

## 2. List the candidates, from their own pages, dated
For each: what they offer, who can sign up (developer programmes, approval processes), the cost, the terms that matter (redistribution, attribution, caching, liability), and how long approval takes. For example:
- **Filing and official briefings:** Leidos Flight Service's developer access.
- **Drone airspace authorisation:** the FAA's list of approved LAANC UAS Service Suppliers, to partner with or to become one.
- **Fuel prices and FBO details:** the FBOs themselves (listings they keep), then licensed feeds for the gaps.
- **Groups of pilots:** flight schools, flying clubs, FBO chains, EAA chapters, Civil Air Patrol wings.

## 3. Weigh them
Cost against the revenue or users it brings (the `business-opportunity` skill), lock-in, what happens if the partner leaves, and whether the app can work without them (build behind an interface, with a stub, until the access exists).

## 4. Prepare the ask
A one-page note for the owner to send: who we are, what the app does for their users or members, what we ask for, what they get (a free school plan, a featured listing, data back), and the next step. Never contact a partner, sign up for a programme or accept terms on the owner's behalf.

## Report
The feature, the candidates with costs, terms and dates, the recommendation, the interface the code builds against meanwhile, and the note for the owner.
